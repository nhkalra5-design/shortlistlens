Switchers rarely lack knowledge. They lack evidence.

  • Certificates show learning, not doing

    A product course proves you studied PM. A shortlister is looking for signs you have already made product decisions.

  • Your bullets describe tasks

    "Built", "maintained", "analysed". Accurate, but they read as execution, which keeps you filed under your current role.

  • Rejections come without reasons

    With no feedback, the default fix is one more course. It does not change how your resume reads.

How it works

Three inputs, one short report. No job description needed.

  1. Pick your switch

    Choose your current role and the role you are applying for.

  2. Paste your top bullets

    Up to 250 words from your resume. Remove names and company details before pasting.

  3. Read your 4-part report

    How you come across, the bullet that pigeonholes you, a rewrite, and one thing to stop doing.

What a report looks like

An illustrative example built around one common engineering bullet. Not a real user's resume.

ShortlistLens / Report Sample
Software engineer, 3 yrs Target: Associate PM

Example bullet

Built APIs for the payments service

1How you come across

A reliable backend engineer who delivers well-scoped work. Not yet someone who decides what gets built, or why.

2The bullet that pigeonholes you

"Built APIs for the payments service"

It names the output and the system, but not the user, the problem or a choice you made. A PM shortlister is likely to read it as execution.

3A rewrite from your real experience

Built the payments service APIs used by [who relied on them]; chose [approach] over [alternative] to [outcome you can verify].

Brackets mark facts only you know. ShortlistLens is designed not to invent numbers or outcomes.

4One thing to stop doing

Stop leading with the system you built. Lead with who it was for and the trade-off you owned.

Every report is designed around the same four parts.

What it won't do, on purpose

A narrow tool is easier to check than a broad one that guesses.

  • Explain why you were rejected

    It cannot see a recruiter's decision. It checks how your resume reads for the next application.

  • Write your resume from scratch

    It rewrites one bullet using only what you paste, and is designed not to invent projects, titles or numbers.

  • Stuff keywords for ATS

    It is written for the human shortlister who decides whether you get a call.

Built for these switches

  • Software engineer Product manager

    You have shipped features. The report shows where your bullets hide the product calls you made along the way.

  • Data or product analyst Product manager

    You find the insight. The report checks whether your resume shows what changed because of it.

  • Business analyst Strategy

    You build the decks and models. The report flags bullets that read as support work rather than recommendations.

Best fit if you have 1 to 5 years of experience in India and are preparing your next round of applications.

Questions

What happens to my resume data?

Your role choices and the bullets you paste are sent to Google's Gemini API to create your report. Each submission and its report are saved in Supabase so I can show a usage count here and improve the product. I don't ask for names, emails or full resumes. I don't sell your data or share it beyond these two services. Remove your name, employer and personal details before pasting.

Is it free?

Yes. The readiness check is free and needs no sign-up.

Will it tell me why I was rejected?

No. Nobody outside the hiring team can know that. ShortlistLens looks forward: how your resume reads for the next role you apply to.

Do I need to paste a job description?

No. You pick a target role, and the report reviews your bullets against what shortlisters for that role typically look for.
